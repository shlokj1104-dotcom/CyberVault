## Key Insight of the Day

Every device connects to the Internet through some form of access network —
and every access network is just a different physical solution to the same
problem: getting bits from your end system to the edge router. DSL reuses
old phone lines. Cable reuses TV infrastructure. Fiber builds new paths.
WiFi and cellular cut the wire entirely. But they all eventually hand off
to the same Internet core. The physical medium determines the speed,
distance, cost, and security characteristics of that connection — which
is why an attacker who understands the medium has a head start.

## Problems I Encountered

### Confusion #1: DSL vs Cable — What's Actually Different?
**Problem:** Both seem to provide home broadband. Why have two technologies?
**Resolution:** They use completely different existing infrastructure:
- DSL uses the **phone company's copper telephone lines** already in homes.
- Cable uses the **cable TV company's coaxial HFC network**.
- DSL is a dedicated point-to-point link — your line is yours only.
- Cable is a **shared broadcast medium** — all neighbors on the same segment
  share bandwidth. This is a key difference for both performance and security.

### Confusion #2: DSLAM vs CMTS — Are They the Same Thing?
**Problem:** Both seemed to do something similar at the ISP side.
**Resolution:** Yes — functionally analogous but for different technologies:
- **DSLAM** (at telco's Central Office) — aggregates DSL signals from many
  copper phone lines, separates data from voice, sends data to Internet.
- **CMTS** (at cable head end) — aggregates signals from many cable modems
  in downstream homes, converts analog to digital, sends data to Internet.
- Same role, different network infrastructure.

### Confusion #3: PON Architecture in FTTH
**Problem:** The ONT → splitter → OLT chain was confusing at first.
**Resolution:** Think of it like this:
- **ONT** = your home's fiber termination box (like a modem).
- **Optical splitter** = a passive device in the neighborhood that splits
  one shared fiber into ~100 individual fibers going to homes.
- **OLT** = at the Central Office; converts optical ↔ electrical signals;
  connects to Internet.
- In PON, packets from OLT are **replicated at the splitter** — similar to
  how cable head end broadcasts to all homes. So FTTH with PON also has a
  shared upstream fiber (though far less congested than cable HFC).

### Confusion #4: Geostationary vs LEO — Why Does Height Matter?
**Problem:** Why does satellite altitude affect latency so much?
**Resolution:** Signal travels at the speed of light (~300,000 km/s).
- Geostationary satellite: 36,000 km up → round trip = ~72,000 km → ~240ms
  just for the signal travel (actual delay is ~280ms total).
- LEO: much closer → lower latency.
- 280ms propagation delay makes geostationary satellites terrible for
  real-time apps (gaming, video calls, VoIP) — but fine for file downloads
  or streaming where a buffer hides the delay.
